The Challenge

The pressure you're being asked to navigate

Governors' offices are asking for statewide AI readiness strategies. Legislators want numbers tied to workforce outcomes. Federal funders are watching to see how states execute on TEGL 03-25.

And consultants are producing frameworks, not delivery. Reports land on desks, but participants don't get trained. Your program directors are left to figure out implementation on their own, with no shared infrastructure and no unified way to report results.

That's the gap AI Essentials fills. We sit between the state strategy and the program-level delivery, providing the curriculum, the assessments, the reporting, and the coordination so every program type in your state is working from the same framework.

The gap isn't strategy. Every state has a strategy. The gap is delivery infrastructure that works across every program type and reports back to one place.
Challenge 01
Federal mandate, fragmented local execution
DOL guidance exists, but implementation is decentralized across dozens of local boards and grantees. There's no shared curriculum, no common reporting, and no way to see the full picture at the state level.
Challenge 02
Frameworks without delivery partners
Consulting firms produce AI workforce strategies. They don't train participants, generate outcome data, or build the infrastructure that makes the strategy defensible in front of a workforce board or legislature.
Challenge 03
Accountability before infrastructure exists
You're being asked to show AI readiness outcomes before the delivery infrastructure to produce them has been built. AI Essentials builds that infrastructure fast, with reporting built in from day one.

Funding Alignment

The money already exists inside your current structure

A statewide AI Essentials deployment doesn't require a new budget line. The federal funding streams your programs already operate inside are authorized for AI literacy training. We help you map the deployment to the right streams so your CFO and grants administrator can sign off with confidence.

WIOA Title I
AI literacy training for adults, dislocated workers, and youth
Explicitly authorized by DOL TEGL 03-25 (August 2025). The largest available funding stream for workforce AI readiness at the state and local level.
WIOA Title II
Adult education and literacy integration
AI literacy components integrate naturally into adult education and English language acquisition programs, supporting participation in the digital workforce.
Perkins V
CTE pathway AI integration and faculty development
Supports AI skill-building within Career and Technical Education programs at the secondary and postsecondary levels, including instructor professional development.
State Funds
State innovation and workforce transformation initiatives
State-level workforce innovation funds, governor's emergency workforce funds, and technology workforce initiatives frequently align with statewide AI readiness deployments.
