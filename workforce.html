The stakes

The jobs most at risk are the ones your participants hold today

The workers WIOA programs serve are disproportionately concentrated in exactly the occupations with the highest automation exposure. Clerical work, data entry, customer service, warehouse operations, and routine manufacturing are all facing significant displacement pressure from AI within the next five years.

AI training doesn't just make someone better at their current job. It moves them from the displacement side of the ledger to the augmentation side, where workers who understand AI tools become more valuable, not less.

AI training doesn't eliminate the risk. It puts the worker on the right side of it.

What a deployment looks like

From intake to credential in six weeks

Here is what a typical WIOA Adult program cohort looks like from start to finish. Every program type follows the same four-step structure, adapted to your population, your schedule, and your reporting cycle.

1
Track selection and pre-assessment
Participants select a sector track based on their career goals. Every learner completes a sector-specific AI readiness baseline that establishes where they start and what they need to gain.
2
Cohort training
12 hours of scenario-based AI training delivered in whatever format fits your program: in-person at an American Job Center, hybrid, or fully online for rural and remote participants.
3
Post-assessment
Every participant completes the post-assessment. Skills gain is calculated per learner and aggregated for the cohort, giving you individual and program-level outcome documentation.
4
Credential and WIOA report
Participants receive their AI-Ready sector certificate. Your program receives a cohort impact report with pre and post assessment data formatted for WIOA Title I performance reporting.
